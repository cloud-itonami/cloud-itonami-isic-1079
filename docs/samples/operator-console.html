What this store does and does not remember

Both statements below are produced by scanning the committed records and the ledger at render time, not written into the page. If approver custody is ever added to seasoningops.store, this section changes on the next build without anyone editing it.

Approver-identity keys found on any committed batch recordnone
Approver-identity keys found anywhere in the audit ledgerby

The audit ledger carries the approval fact, but the committed batch record does not. store/log-batch and store/finalize-shipment take no approver argument — they persist batch metadata and a one-way flag, nothing about who authorised the change. So “who released this batch?” is answerable only by joining the ledger back to the record; the record on its own cannot distinguish a human-approved commit from an unapproved one. Stating this rather than quietly omitting the approver is the point: a reader must be able to tell “nobody approved” from “the store did not keep it”.

Second finding, also measured: the shipped governor/hold-fact writes a permanent refusal and a soft escalation under the same :t :governor-hold / :disposition :hold, and does not carry the verdict's :hard? or :escalate? flags into the fact. This run produced 19 hard and 4 soft holds; reading the ledger alone, the only thing separating them is that a soft hold's :basis is empty. That is an implicit discriminator, not a recorded one. Left as found — changing it is a governance decision with its own contract tests.

Third: operation/run-operation returns {:ok? true :facts []} on the pass path — no commit fact and no updated store — so the :auto-commit and :operator-signoff facts above are written by the scenario driver in seasoningops.render-html, not by the shipped actor. The :governor-hold facts are the shipped actor's own output. Distinct hard rules exercised in this run: 19.
